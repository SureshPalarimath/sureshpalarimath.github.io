Teaching

Eighteen years of teaching computing at diploma, undergraduate and postgraduate levels in three countries.

Teaching philosophy

Students learn computing best when they build working systems from real data and are accountable for how those systems behave. Over 18 years of teaching in Oman, Papua New Guinea and India, I have found that understanding grows fastest when theory, hands-on practice and reflection are linked within each course.

Learning by building. In AI, machine learning and data science courses, each topic has a short concept session, a guided laboratory and an applied mini project. Students see a method demonstrated, implement it in Python with standard libraries, and then apply it to a dataset with a clear question to answer.

Connecting theory with practice. I design assessment around tasks professionals actually perform: cleaning and exploring data, building and validating models, explaining results to non-specialists and documenting decisions. Responsible AI runs through every course, so students discuss bias, privacy and explainability whenever they evaluate a model.

Inclusive learning. My students come from many linguistic and educational backgrounds. I use Moodle for structured materials, formative quizzes and timely feedback, and I hold regular advising sessions on progression, project choice and careers. My research on personalized learning and assistive technology shapes how I adapt material and remove barriers to participation.

Research-informed mentoring. I bring undergraduate and postgraduate students into funded research, and several of these projects have led to student co-authored publications and conference presentations.
