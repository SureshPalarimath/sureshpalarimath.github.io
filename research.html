Research

Applied AI, machine learning and IoT for education, campus sustainability and accessibility, built and evaluated with real users.

Research statement

My research applies artificial intelligence, machine learning and the Internet of Things to problems in education, campus sustainability and accessibility. The common thread is the design of data-driven systems that are deployed and evaluated with real users, rather than tested only on benchmark data.

I have led seven funded projects from MoHERI and UTAS, and I involve undergraduate and postgraduate students in each one. Several of these projects have produced student co-authored papers and conference presentations.

Current themes

AI in education and personalized learning

AI-based learning systems that adapt content and pacing to individual learners, and conversational AI that supports students in higher education. This work combines learner data, machine learning models and evaluation in real courses, and includes the AIPLI framework for equitable learning in low- and middle-income countries.

Smart and sustainable campus systems

IoT and AI solutions for monitoring and managing energy and resources on campus, aimed at efficiency and sustainability targets. This theme grew from the MoHERI-funded Smart Sustainable Green UTAS Campus project.

AI and IoT assistive technologies

Assistive systems for visually impaired users that combine sensing, embedded devices and machine learning, including a smart reader and AI-enabled door accessibility, to improve independent mobility and access to information.

Applied machine learning

Supervised and deep learning for classification, prediction and decision support in healthcare, security, agriculture and computer vision, with attention to model validation and practical deployment.

Featured project: AIPLI

AIPLI (AI-Powered Personalized Learning Inclusion) is a research-grade adaptive learning platform designed for low- and middle-income countries and aligned with UN Sustainable Development Goal 4. It runs entirely in the browser without a server, so schools with limited infrastructure can use it.

The platform combines Bayesian Knowledge Tracing, multiple-intelligence profiling, spaced repetition and explainable AI. It includes an educator dashboard, a learning path engine, subject modules for science, mathematics and language, and a dataset analyser with an equity audit. The design, development and pilot evaluation are published in the International Journal of Drug Delivery Technology (2026).
